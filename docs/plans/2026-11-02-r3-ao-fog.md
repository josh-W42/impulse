# R3 — Ambient Occlusion and Fog: Plan + Results

**Date:** 2026-11-02 · **Status:** hand-checked and merged (PR #7) · **Track:**
[render quality](2026-11-02-render-quality-track.md)

## Why

Ambient light reaches every point equally, so objects float and corners
look flat; only glTF occlusion textures darken anything. Nothing fades
with distance either, so large scenes have no depth cue.

## Design

```
shadows ─► prepass (normals + depth, 1×) ─► GTAO ─► 4×4 blur ─► AO texture
                                                                  │
                         scene pass (MSAA): ambient × AO, then fog ◄┘ ─► post
```

- **Prepass:** the lit vertex shaders plus a tiny fragment shader. It
  writes world normals (`Rgba8Unorm`) and depth (`Depth32Float`) at one
  sample per pixel. The main pass can't provide these: its depth is
  multisampled, and WebGL2 can't sample that. Cost: geometry drawn once
  more.
- **AO:** ground-truth AO (Jimenez et al. 2016), at full resolution, in
  view space:
  - 2 slices × 4 steps per side, a 1 m radius by default, and a linear
    falloff over the outer 40%.
  - The slice rotation and step jitter follow a 4×4 pattern. A 4×4
    depth-aware blur averages it out exactly, so no TAA is needed.
  - The camera basis and pixels-per-metre come from `view_proj` on the
    CPU (`ao.rs`, unit-tested). `CameraData` is unchanged.
- **Applying AO:** the lit shader reads the AO texel at its pixel
  (group 3). AO multiplies ambient diffuse and specular only, never the
  sun, with Jimenez's multi-bounce fit so bright albedo doesn't go grey.
  It combines with the glTF occlusion texture by `min`.
- **Fog:** exponential height fog with Quilez's analytic integral along
  the view ray. It fades toward the sky's horizon colour plus some sun
  in-scatter, so distant ground meets the background sky without a seam.
  Applied in the lit shader before exposure; the sky itself is unfogged.
- **API:**
  - `AoSettings { enabled, radius, intensity, show }` and
    `FogSettings { enabled, density, height_falloff }`, each with a
    setter.
  - Both are on by default; fog is subtle (about 10% at 30 m on the
    ground).
  - `show` draws the AO buffer alone (debug).
- **WebGL2:** everything is fragment shaders, `textureLoad` on a depth
  texture, and renderable 8-bit formats.
- **Sandbox:** the Rendering section moves into a collapsible header and
  gains AO and fog controls.

## Tasks

   `ao.wgsl` (GTAO, blur) with naga WebGL2 checks.
2. AO targets and passes; prepass pipelines (static and skinned); lit
   pipelines take group 3.
3. `scene.wgsl`: AO lookup, multi-bounce, fog, AO debug view.
4. API and sandbox panel.
5. fmt, clippy (native + wasm), tests, `cargo check` solstice and Ashen
   Crown.
6. Verify WebGPU, WebGL2, native (AO debug view, fog), skinned example.

## Results

Built as designed, with two changes found while verifying:

- **The prepass writes linear view depth to an `R32Float` colour target
  instead of the AO shader sampling the depth buffer.**
  - **naga:** its GLSL backend can't `textureLoad` a `texture_depth_2d`.
    The WebGL2 shader test caught that.
  - **WebGL2:** binding the depth texture as unfilterable float got past
    naga, but on WebGL2 it read back 0 everywhere. A debug build showing
    1 m depth stripes made that visible (the floor's AO came out uniformly
    grey).
  - **The fix:** the AO shader now rebuilds positions from linear depth and
    a pixels-per-metre scale, with no matrix inverse and no depth-texture
    sampling.
  - **Support:** `R32Float` renders wherever the HDR path does (the same
    `EXT_color_buffer_float`). `SceneTarget::ao` records it, and
    `Renderer::ao_supported()` reports it. Without it, AO is off.
- **The sandbox's Rendering section starts collapsed.** Open, the panel was
  taller than a 600 px viewport.

Not done:
- **Frame-time cost not measured.** The prepass draws all geometry a
  second time, and GTAO plus the blur run at full resolution. GPU
  timestamps are still owed (R1–R3).
- **AO strength not tuned against real assets.** Defaults are 1 m radius,
  intensity 1; it reads as subtle.
