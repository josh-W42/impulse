# M5 — Scale: Design + Lean Plan

**Date:** 2026-09-26 · **Status:** approved · **Parent:** `2026-09-24-impulse-engine-design.md`

## Design

- **Broadphase: dynamic AABB tree** (Box2D lineage): fat AABBs (margin) so bodies are
  re-inserted only when they leave them; surface-area-heuristic-free cheap insertion
  (sibling by least enlargement) with AVL-style rotations or rebalancing on insert.
  Infinite planes stay out of the tree and are tested against every dynamic body.
  Rejected: sweep-and-prune (degrades when many bodies overlap on the sort axis, e.g.
  tall piles) and uniform grid (tuning, poor with varied sizes).
- **Sleeping with islands:** union-find over contacts each step; an island sleeps when
  all its bodies stay below speed thresholds for 0.5 s; sleeping bodies skip
  integration and solving; wake on contact with an awake body or on reset.
- **Profiler without breaking the glam-only rule:** `World::step_profiled(dt, clock)`
  takes an engine-supplied clock (`std` timers panic on wasm) and returns per-phase
  timings + counts (pairs, contacts, awake/asleep).
- **Benchmarks:** `cargo run --release -p impulse-physics --example bench` (native
  step times at 100/500/1000 bodies) + browser frame times under the telemetry-viewer
  discipline (visible tab, no mid-run polling). Results in `BENCHMARKS.md`, including
  before (brute force) vs after.
- **Demo:** "Rain" scene, 1,000 mixed bodies in a larger pit; target 60 fps in browser.

## Tasks

**P0. Baseline** — bench example; record brute-force numbers ("before").
**P1. AABBs** per collider (sphere, oriented box; planes infinite). Tests vs corners.
**P2. Dynamic AABB tree** — insert/remove/move (fat margin), pair query.
Tests: pairs == brute force on random scenes incl. after moves/removals; height bound.
**P3. Broadphase in `find_contacts`** — tree for finite shapes, planes vs dynamics.
Test: contacts identical to brute force on random scenes; all prior tests pass.
**P4. Islands + sleeping** — tests: resting stack sleeps < 2 s; ball wakes a sleeping
stack; sleeping bodies don't move; determinism.
**P5. Profiler** — `step_profiled`, counts; panel readout.
**P6. Rain scene + BENCHMARKS.md** ("after" native + browser).
**P7. Verify + ship** (full backend matrix, native debug with user).

## Results

**Before (brute-force O(n²) pairs; native, release, `--example bench`, 300 steps):**

| bodies | mean step (ms) | p95 step (ms) | max step (ms) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 0.42 | 0.55 | 0.76 |
| 500 | 4.46 | 5.02 | 5.73 |
| 1000 | 14.10 | 14.99 | 16.92 |

10× bodies → 33× time: the O(n²) pair loop dominates.

**After broadphase (P3; same benchmark):**

| bodies | mean step (ms) | p95 step (ms) | max step (ms) | vs brute force |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 0.32 | 0.38 | 0.43 | 1.3× |
| 500 | 1.75 | 2.24 | 2.96 | 2.5× |
| 1000 | 3.15 | 3.75 | 4.32 | 4.5× |

Near-linear scaling (10× bodies → 10× time). Contacts are bit-identical to brute force
(equivalence test on a random 200-body scene over 3 rounds of simulation). Without the
AVL rotations, sorted insertion degenerates into a chain (height 1023 vs ≤ 15).

**After sleeping (P4) and final results:** see `BENCHMARKS.md`. Native 1,000 bodies:
14.10 ms (brute force) → 3.15 ms (broadphase) → 1.24 ms mean (sleeping; p95 3.43 ms).
Browser, Rain scene (1,000 bodies): locked 60 fps on WebGPU and WebGL2, falling and
settled; profiler shows ~2.9 ms physics per frame.

- Tests: tree pairs == brute force through inserts/moves/removals; AVL keeps sorted
  insertion at height ≤ 15 (1023 without rotations — mutation-checked); contacts
  bit-identical with/without broadphase; stacks sleep and stay bit-identical; a ball
  wakes a sleeping stack (mutation-checked); sleeping can be disabled; falling bodies
  never sleep.
- Known simplification: waking a sleeping island cold-starts its contacts (warm-start
  impulses aren't kept for sleeping pairs).
- `?scene=` URL parameter (e.g. `?scene=rain`, `?scene=tower`).
- User verified native debug: Rain, profiler readout, sleeping toggle, tower goes
  fully still when asleep, other scenes unchanged.
